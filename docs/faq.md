# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is FACF a cryptocurrency or mining network?

No. FACF coordinates useful AI workloads. The MVP uses ordinary contracts, invoices, and ledger reconciliation and has no token or proof-of-work mechanism.

## Can anyone join by installing the software?

Anyone may run the open software subject to its license. Joining a particular network requires that network operator's enrollment and policies. The official managed network is controlled by Cloud Computing Oy.

## Can a laptop participate?

Yes, when its owner explicitly enables capacity and local safety limits are satisfied. Consumer devices are initially suitable for development, community-tier, synthetic, or public-data workloads—not arbitrary confidential business data.

## Does FACF combine thousands of GPUs into one giant model?

Not in the MVP. It federates whole requests or bounded batches to compatible execution cells. Splitting model layers over unrelated internet hosts would impose difficult latency, reliability, and security constraints.

## How is confidential data protected?

By workload classification, trust policies, private fabrics, minimal metadata, contractual controls, and—where supported and verified—confidential-computing attestation. Ordinary encryption in transit does not hide plaintext from a normal execution host.

## Why would customers use the managed network instead of a fork?

The managed service supplies verified supply, policy enforcement, a single commercial relationship, settlement, support, SLA options, and accumulated operational reputation. The open protocol remains useful and auditable.

## How does Cloud Computing Oy make money?

Potential revenue includes managed usage margin, subscriptions, private-fabric licenses and operations, verified-provider programs, enterprise support, and integration services. These are hypotheses until validated through paid pilots.

## Is FACF production-ready?

No. The repository currently defines the product, protocol direction, commercial structure, and MVP gates. Software and production claims must follow implementation, security testing, pilots, and measured operations.
